# D1.2 B — Graphic Split

Uses [the locked D1 fixture](D1_FIXTURE.md) without changing its four jobs, role context, actions, or temporary palette. This is one composition study, not a selected design.

1. **Design hypothesis:** A more graphic foreground can signal FreelaX's work identity while still making title, budget, state, and next action immediately legible.
2. **Composition grammar:** The focus job is an asymmetric split: a flat vermilion factual panel and a wider bone reading field. Ink boundaries define the split. Secondary jobs remain unboxed ruled rows, preventing a poster block from becoming a card-for-everything template.
3. **Information hierarchy:** Screen identity and supported search controls → focus title/description → client and USD budget → `OPEN` / **Chưa ứng tuyển** → **Ứng tuyển**. The same three other jobs follow in the same order. The colored panel is an anchor, not a new data group.
4. **Typography behavior:** System sans with a bold, compact title set against ample line space. Numeric budget uses tabular figures and a larger step than metadata. UI labels stay conventional and readable; no final font is chosen.
5. **Shape language:** One high-priority rectangular split and one restrained hard offset shadow on its action. Straight ink dividers support the rest. No soft shadow, ubiquitous border box, or pill system.
6. **Color-block behavior:** Shared temporary Bone / Ink / Vermilion / Acid Yellow / Sage palette. Vermilion is committed to the focus panel; Acid Yellow punctuates the small status marker. Secondary information returns to Bone so color does not overwhelm the list. Tokens remain exploratory.
7. **CTA treatment:** **Ứng tuyển** sits inside the focus composition as the single high-contrast action. Secondary rows use the same action wording with lower visual weight. Client proof shows **Xem ứng tuyển** as owner action, title/description edit as secondary, and cancellation as a quiet tertiary path.
8. **State treatment at D1.2 scope:** `OPEN` and **Chưa ứng tuyển** stay explicit in words. The graphic block does not imply application success. Other job/application states are only future D1.3 work.
9. **Desktop behavior:** The split occupies a substantial but bounded width; list rows begin directly below it. Client proof reuses the split at smaller scale, with its owner context and actions aligned to the right of job facts.
10. **Mobile behavior:** The split becomes a vertical sequence: state/budget panel first, then title and description, then CTA. Rules and text maintain a single reading order at 390px and 320px; no fixed-width panels.
11. **Intentionally avoids:** School Safety poster imitation, independent colored boxes for every row, crypto/wallet identity, applicant counts, company claims, and editable budget.
12. **Strengths:** Strong visual recognition and a clear action owner; facts remain anchored while the title stays dominant.
13. **Risks:** The colored focus panel may dominate repeated visits or bury additional jobs. At scale, contrast between focus and list needs calibration.
14. **D1.3 tests:** Applied-state grammar without extra badges, long title wrapping inside the split, focus and keyboard states, empty/error results, client owner actions with loaded applicants, and whether the composition tolerates more than four jobs.
15. **Prototype path:** `paypal/docs/ui/d1/prototypes/study_b_graphic_split.html`.
